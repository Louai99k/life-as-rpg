## Setting Premise

World is present-day Earth with normal human society, technology, geography, and institutions intact. Supernatural forces and System-selected Players first emerge through confusion, secrecy, and fragmented awareness, then become increasingly visible, understood, and consequential at wider social scale.

## Real-World Baseline

- Real cities, laws, economics, and daily life remain default frame.
- Human bodies, tools, and environments follow real-world expectations unless rules explicitly say otherwise.
- Supernatural events are intrusions into normal reality, not replacement for it.

## Supernatural Overlay

- System selects certain individuals as Players.
- Players receive private panels and progression mechanics.
- Fantasy powers, enemies, and mission structures enter world through System logic.
- Public awareness begins with misunderstanding and partial visibility.
- Over time, world does not stay ignorant; institutions and societies increasingly recognize that something real is happening.

## What Players Know

- They have been selected.
- They can gain power.
- Missions, rewards, and progression are real.
- Some threats and opportunities now operate under hidden logic beyond normal society.
- Broader world understanding may lag behind Player understanding at first, but gap does not remain permanent.

## What Remains Hidden

- Full origin of System.
- Total number of Players.
- True long-term goal of supernatural intrusion.
- Upper limit of world transformation.
- Full political, military, and social consequences of System integration during early phase.

## Tone Rules

- Tone should balance mundane realism with escalating supernatural intensity.
- Scenes should feel grounded before they feel spectacular.
- Power fantasy is valid, but stakes must stay legible.
- Documentation tone should stay precise, not melodramatic.

## Genre Rules

- Urban fantasy: modern life plus supernatural layer that begins hidden or denied, then moves toward wider recognition and integration.
- LitRPG: explicit stats, progression, rewards, and game-like structures.
- Solo play: rules must support one decision-maker without GM overhead.
- Anime influence is valid for power style, but system language must remain clear and playable.

## Content Boundaries

- Keep focus on playable mechanics, not unrestricted lore sprawl.
- Avoid worldbuilding that creates rules obligations without payoff.
- Do not assume medieval fantasy structures like guild halls, dungeon towns, or kingdoms unless the shape of the world itself changes because of the Players and System.

## Example Atmosphere

Player leaves normal daily routine, receives mission panel, discovers impossible threat in familiar place, resolves conflict through explicit mechanics, and returns to ordinary world that is slowly shifting from denial into recognition.

## Canon Rules

- Earth is real and modern.
- System is real and active.
- Player growth is measurable.
- Supernatural content can scale from subtle to extreme.
- Public understanding of supernatural reality evolves over time; secrecy is early-phase condition, not permanent truth.
- Rules canon overrides flavor when conflict appears.
